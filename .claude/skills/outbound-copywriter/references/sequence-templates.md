# Sequence templates

Read when writing anything past the opener. Each step has exactly one job, and a step that cannot name its job should not exist.

---

## Step 1 - The opener (day 0)

**Job:** earn the reply. Prove relevance. Ask one question.

Use SPARK, one archetype, under 120 words.

```
Subject: {{company}} + [topic]

[Personalized opening, one or two lines, referencing a signal, a peer or an observation]

[Agitate, two or three lines naming their pain in their language]

[Value, one specific proof point with a number]

[CTA, one low-friction question]

[First name]
```

---

## Step 2 - The value-add (day 3 to 4)

**Job:** provide new value and read the interest signal.

Do not reference step 1. Write it as though step 1 never happened, with a new angle and new value. A follow-up that points back at the first email tells the reader they already ignored you once.

```
Subject: (same thread)

[One-line context reset, a new angle or a new proof point]

[Binary question that is easy to answer]

P.S. [Social proof with a specific metric]
```

Example:

```
Subject: (same thread)

Put together a breakdown of how [peer company] restructured their outbound
last quarter. They went from 12 meetings a month to 41 without adding headcount.

Want a copy, or is outbound already working the way you need it to?

P.S. They were running the same two tools you are.
```

The P.S. is prime real estate from step 2 onwards, because it gets read even when the body is skimmed. One metric, no second CTA.

---

## Step 3 - The reframe (day 7 to 10)

**Job:** try a genuinely different angle. If steps 1 and 2 were about the problem, step 3 is about curiosity or a micro-commitment.

```
Subject: (same thread)

[Completely new angle: a different pain, a different proof, or a question]

[Micro-commitment CTA: a two-minute walkthrough, a one-page teardown, a single data point]
```

Example:

```
Subject: (same thread)

One thing I keep hearing from [industry] VPs is that the bottleneck is not lead
volume, it is reply quality. Teams book meetings with people who were never
going to buy.

We put together a two-minute walkthrough on filtering for buying intent before the
first email. Worth watching, or is this not a priority right now?
```

---

## Step 4 - The breakup (day 14 to 21)

**Job:** give them a clean exit. Breakups convert well, and a meaningful share of total positive replies arrive here, so do not skip the step and do not half-write it.

```
Subject: (same thread)

[Short, human, direct. No guilt trip.]

[Numbered options: 1. Not interested 2. Bad timing 3. Wrong person]

Either way, no hard feelings.

[First name]
```

Example:

```
Subject: (same thread)

Hey {{firstName}},

Not trying to be a pest, so I will check once more and then stop.

1. You are all set, not something you need help with
2. Timing is off, worth circling back in a few months
3. Wrong person, happy to be pointed at the right one

No worries either way.

[Your name]
```

Numbered options work because they lower the cost of replying from composing a paragraph to typing a digit, and a "2" is a dated re-entry point rather than a dead end.

---

## Step 5 - The long game (day 30 to 60), optional

**Job:** re-engage with something genuinely new: a new case study, a benchmark report, a real industry shift.

Only send this if the new value exists. If you are rephrasing steps 1 to 4, stop at step 4. A fifth email with nothing in it costs you the fourth one's goodwill, and it is the step most likely to earn a spam complaint.
