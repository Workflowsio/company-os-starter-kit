# Call type playbooks

Read at step 1, before any research. The call type decides which research layers matter and how the page is structured, and getting it wrong wastes the whole prep.

---

## First call (discovery)

**Goal:** validate the pain hypotheses and earn a second conversation.

- **Prep emphasis:** layers 1 and 2. Layer 4 if there is time.
- **Talking ratio:** you 30 percent, them 70 percent.
- **Structure:** rapport 2 min, context-setting 1 min, discovery 15 to 20 min, a brief value statement 3 min, next steps 2 min.
- **Question focus:** current state, where it breaks down, and what good would look like for them.
- **Do not:** pitch features, show slides, demo anything, or raise pricing unless they do.
- **Success:** they agree to a second meeting with a clear reason for it.

The goal of a first call is almost never to close. It is to understand the situation, test the hypotheses and earn the right to a second conversation.

---

## Follow-up or second call

**Goal:** deepen the understanding, widen to other stakeholders, and build the business case.

- **Prep emphasis:** re-read the notes from call one. Research any new stakeholder. Layer 3 becomes the important one here.
- **Structure:** recap 2 min and confirm you heard them right, deeper discovery on the specific pain 10 min, tailored solution 10 min, concerns 5 min, next steps 3 min.
- **Key questions:** "Last time you mentioned X, has anything changed?" and "Who else on your team does this affect?" and "What would solving it be worth to your team?"
- **Do not:** repeat discovery questions from call one. They notice, and it undoes the credit the first call earned.
- **Success:** a clear next step, being a demo, a proposal, or an introduction to the decision maker.

---

## Demo

**Goal:** show exactly how this solves their specific problem. Not a feature tour.

- **Prep emphasis:** map their stated pains to specific capabilities, and prepare two or three scenarios using their own language and their own examples.
- **Structure:** recap their situation 2 min, solve pain one 8 min, solve pain two 8 min, questions 10 min, next steps 2 min.
- **Key questions:** "Does this match how your team would actually use it?" and "What would you change about what you just saw?"
- **Do not:** show every feature, click through settings, demo anything they did not ask about, or say "and another thing it does" more than once.
- **Success:** they ask for pricing, or ask when they could start.

---

## Negotiation or proposal review

**Goal:** agree terms, remove the last blockers, get to yes.

- **Prep emphasis:** competitive positioning from layer 3, the contract terms, the return calculation, and coaching for your internal champion.
- **Structure:** confirm mutual interest 1 min, walk the proposal 10 min, objections one at a time 15 min, agree terms or the next step 5 min.
- **Key questions:** "What would need to be true for you to move forward this month?" and "Is there anybody else who needs to sign off?"
- **Do not:** discount straight away, rush past an objection, or read silence as agreement.
- **Success:** a signed contract, or a clear timeline with a named blocker attached.

---

## Renewal or expansion

**Goal:** show the value delivered, then widen the relationship.

- **Prep emphasis:** usage data, the results from their engagement, what has shipped since they signed, and how their needs have moved.
- **Structure:** review the wins together 5 min, share the metrics 5 min, discuss what is next for their team 10 min, present the expansion 5 min, next steps 5 min.
- **Key questions:** "What has been most valuable so far?" and "What are your priorities next quarter?" and "Is there a team next to yours with the same problem?"
- **Do not:** treat the renewal as done, skip the value review, or pitch expansion before confirming they are satisfied.
- **Success:** the renewal is confirmed, or an expansion conversation is scheduled.

---

## Rules that hold across every type

- Silence is a tool. After a question, wait. They will fill it with something you would never have got by talking.
- Take notes during and update the CRM within ten minutes after, because memory decays fast and a great brief is wasted if the follow-up is generic.
- With multiple stakeholders, write a separate short profile for each. Different roles carry different pains and different decision criteria.
- If you are talking more than 40 percent of the time on a discovery call, you are talking too much.
- Send a short agenda 24 hours ahead. It reduces no-shows and sets the expectation of what the call is for.
- Send the follow-up within two hours: what you discussed, the agreed next step, and anything you promised.
