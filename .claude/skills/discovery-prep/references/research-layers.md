# The four research layers

Read at step 2. Layers 1 and 2 for a 10-minute prep, all four for a high-value meeting. Source columns name categories rather than specific products, because products change and this file will not.

---

## Layer 1: Company

**Goal:** understand what this company does, how big it is and what stage it is at.

| Data point | Where to find it | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| What they sell | Homepage and product pages | Grounds every talking point in their reality rather than yours |
| Headcount | Company page, enrichment | Decides the pains, the buying process and the budget |
| Revenue, if public | Funding databases, filings, press | Sets deal size and budget expectations |
| Funding history | Funding databases, enrichment | Recent funding means budget plus pressure to deploy it |
| Markets and geography | About page, job postings | Shapes how you position |
| Business model | Website, company page | B2B, B2C and marketplace are three different conversations |
| Recent news | News search, company blog, press page | Proves you did homework, and supplies the opener |
| Tech stack | Technographic tools, job postings | Shows what they already run and where the gap might be |
| Likely buying committee | Company page filtered by department, annual reports | Maps the committee before the call rather than during it |
| Financial health signals | Filings, hiring pace, employer reviews | Growing, flat or contracting changes what they will approve |

---

## Layer 2: Person

**Goal:** understand who you are talking to, what they care about and how they communicate. This is the layer people skip, and the person makes the decision rather than the company.

| Data point | Where to find it | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Title and tenure | Professional profile | How long they have been in role shapes their priorities entirely |
| Previous roles | Professional profile | The career path shows what they value and where they are expert |
| Recent activity | Their feed | What they care about right now, in their own words |
| Mutual connections | Professional network | A possible warm reference point |
| Content they published | Their feed, company blog, podcasts, conference talks | Quoting somebody back to themselves builds rapport faster than anything else on this page |
| Their team structure | Company page filtered by department | Who else is likely in the committee |
| Communication style | Their posts, the email thread | Formal or casual, data-driven or narrative. Mirror it |
| Recent job change or promotion | Professional profile, job-change feeds | New in role means new priorities, openness to vendors and a need for a quick win |

Quote their own words back to them. If they wrote about a challenge, reference it. If they said something in a prior email, bring it up. Nothing else in this file builds rapport as fast, and it costs two minutes.

---

## Layer 3: Competitive landscape

**Goal:** know who else they might be evaluating, using or comparing you against.

| Data point | Where to find it | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Tools they use in your category | Technographic tools, job postings, their case studies | If they already have something, you need a displacement angle rather than a pitch |
| Who they are evaluating | Review sites, or asked directly in a prior conversation | Lets you position against the right alternative rather than all of them |
| Why customers switch | Your own win/loss analysis, competitor reviews | Gives specific talking points rather than generic differentiation |
| Analyst coverage | Analyst reports and grids | Useful for enterprise buyers who rely on external validation |
| Competitor pricing model | Public pricing, your team's intelligence | Lets you frame price before the question arrives |
| Whether they are building it in-house | Job postings for that function | If they are building, your pitch is speed and focus rather than features |
| Contract renewal timing | Prior conversations, enrichment | Mid-contract shifts the timeline, near-renewal creates urgency |

---

## Layer 4: Recent signals

**Goal:** find the time-sensitive events that create relevance or urgency.

| Signal | Where to find it | How to use it |
|---|---|---|
| New executive in a buying role | Professional profiles, job-change feeds | New leaders bring new priorities and are the most open window you get |
| Funding round | Funding databases, press | New capital means budget plus pressure to deploy it |
| Job postings for roles you support | Job boards, careers page | Hiring shows what they are investing in next |
| Product launch or major update | Their blog, launch platforms, press | Congratulate, then tie your value to the new direction |
| Earnings or public filing | Filings, earnings calls | For public companies, this is their stated priority list |
| Event participation | Speaker lists, their feed | "I saw you spoke about X" is a strong, specific opener |
| Layoffs or restructuring | Press, employer reviews, professional network | Sensitive. Do not raise it. It tells you budget is tight and priorities moved |
| Customer wins or awards | Their blog, press, their feed | Congratulate, then ask what is next |
| Executive departure | Professional profiles, press | May mean a strategy shift, a budget freeze, or a new decision maker arriving |
| Negative press or a product incident | News search, public forums | Do not raise it. It tells you their headspace and it may create urgency |

The last two rows and the layoffs row are context rather than material. Write them into the brief with the marker attached, because a rep who reads them as talking points does real damage in the first minute.

## Source quick reference

| What you need | Free | Paid |
|---|---|---|
| Company overview | Professional networks, funding databases free tier, their website | Funding databases, contact databases |
| Person profile | Professional profile, search | Sales-grade professional network seats, contact databases |
| Tech stack | Job postings, free technographic tools | Enrichment platforms, technographic tools |
| Funding data | Funding database free tier, press | Funding databases, market intelligence platforms |
| Intent signals | Their public activity, job postings, news alerts | Intent data providers, enrichment platforms |
| Competitive intelligence | Review sites, competitor websites | Competitive intelligence platforms |
| News and events | News search, their blog, public feeds | Media monitoring platforms |
| CRM history | Your CRM | n/a |
| Prior threads | Your inbox and shared inboxes | n/a |
| Buying committee | Professional network, annual reports | Sales-grade network seats, org chart tools |

Free sources cover most briefs. Exhaust them before spending, and name what is actually missing before proposing a paid lookup.
