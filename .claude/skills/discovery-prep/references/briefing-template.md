# The briefing template

Read at step 4 while assembling, and at step 5 before handing over. Fill it, then cut it to one page. The rep has four minutes and reads the top third.

---

```
============================================================
CALL BRIEFING
============================================================

MEETING:   [date] [time] [duration]
PROSPECT:  [name], [title]
COMPANY:   [company]
CALL TYPE: first call / follow-up / demo / negotiation / renewal
SOURCE:    how they entered the pipeline
OUTCOME:   what you want to walk away with

============================================================
30-SECOND VERSION
============================================================
- They are a [size] [industry] company that [what they do].
- You are talking to [name], [title], [tenure].
- They most likely care about [top pain hypothesis].
- Open with: [one specific reference to their world].
- Ask: [the one question that matters most].
- Propose: [the specific next step] at the end.

------------------------------------------------------------
1. COMPANY SNAPSHOT
------------------------------------------------------------
- What they do: [one or two sentences]
- Size: [headcount], [revenue if known]
- Stage: [bootstrapped / seed / series X / public]
- Markets: [geographies or verticals]
- Recent news: [one or two relevant items, dated]
- Likely buying committee: [names and titles]

------------------------------------------------------------
2. PERSON PROFILE
------------------------------------------------------------
- Role: [title], in role since [date]
- Background: [previous companies and roles, one or two sentences]
- Recent activity: [what they post about, anything notable and dated]
- Communication style: [formal or casual, data-driven or narrative]
- Mutual connections: [names, if any]
- Personal hooks: [something they wrote, a talk they gave, a milestone]

------------------------------------------------------------
3. PAIN HYPOTHESES (hypotheses, not facts)
------------------------------------------------------------
- Pain 1: [the problem] | Evidence: [what you actually observed]
- Pain 2: [the problem] | Evidence: [what you actually observed]
- Pain 3: [the problem] | Evidence: [what you actually observed]

The call's job is to validate or disprove these. Lead with curiosity.
Mark any hypothesis resting on a single weak signal as weak.

------------------------------------------------------------
4. TALKING POINTS
------------------------------------------------------------
- Open with: [something specific: their content, a mutual connection,
  a company event. Never "tell me about your role".]
- Bridge to pain: [the transition question. "I saw you recently [signal].
  A lot of [role] are telling us [pain]. Are you seeing the same?"]
- Value, if asked: [one sentence. "We help [who] achieve [outcome]
  without [the pain they feel today]."]
- Proof point: [one example, cleared for use, with the metric and timeframe]

------------------------------------------------------------
5. QUESTIONS
------------------------------------------------------------
Discovery
  1. Walk me through how you handle [the area] today.
  2. What works about that? Where does it break down?
  3. If you could fix one thing about it, what would it be?
  4. What has that cost you, in time, revenue or headcount?

Qualification
  5. Who else would be involved in evaluating something like this?
  6. What does your timeline look like for making a change?
  7. Have you looked at other options? What did you like or not like?
  8. Is there budget for this, or would we build the case together?

Closing
  9. Based on this, would it make sense to [specific next step]?
  10. What would need to happen on your end before that?

By call type
  - Follow-up:   Last time you mentioned [X]. Has anything changed?
  - Demo:        Does this match how your team would actually use it?
  - Negotiation: What is the one thing that would make this obvious?
  - Renewal:     What has been most valuable over the past [period]?

------------------------------------------------------------
6. OBJECTIONS TO EXPECT
------------------------------------------------------------
[the two or three most likely, from the library below]

------------------------------------------------------------
7. COMPETITIVE POSITIONING
------------------------------------------------------------
If they mention [competitor A]: [your differentiator, one sentence, verified]
If they mention [competitor B]: [your differentiator, one sentence, verified]
If they are building in-house:  [why buying beats building at their stage]
If they say nobody else:        [reframe around the cost of the status quo]

------------------------------------------------------------
8. CONTEXT, NOT MATERIAL (do not raise these)
------------------------------------------------------------
- [layoffs, negative press, an executive departure, anything sensitive]
- These shape the headspace they arrive in. They are not talking points.

------------------------------------------------------------
9. NEXT STEP TO PROPOSE
------------------------------------------------------------
One of:
- [ ] Send a tailored follow-up with [specific resource]
- [ ] Schedule a demo with [their team plus your specialist]
- [ ] Share the proposal
- [ ] Introduce them to [a customer reference in their industry]
- [ ] Pilot with defined success criteria
- [ ] Follow-up with [the additional stakeholder they named]

Confirm who, what and when: "I will send [X] by [date], and let's book
[the next meeting] for [day]. Does [day and time] work?"

------------------------------------------------------------
10. WHAT WAS NOT CHECKED
------------------------------------------------------------
- [layers skipped, and why. A skipped layer is not a clean layer.]

============================================================
```

## Objection library

The shipped rows are a starting library. Add every objection you hear on a live call, with the response that actually worked, and date it.

| Objection | Why they say it | Response |
|---|---|---|
| "We already have a solution for this" | Status quo bias, fear of switching cost | "That makes sense. What made you take this meeting despite having something in place?" Forces them to name the gap themselves |
| "The timing isn't right" | No urgency, or competing priorities | Tie it to a signal: "You mentioned [X] is a priority. What changes if this waits another quarter?" |
| "I need to talk to my team" | Not the sole decision maker | "Understood. Would a one-pager they can review help? Who specifically should I tailor it for?" |
| "Send me more info" | A polite brush-off, or a real need | "Happy to. What would be most useful: a case study, a pricing overview, or a walkthrough?" If they cannot say, it was a brush-off and you now know |
| "It's too expensive" | Budget constraint, or unclear return | "Too expensive relative to what: your current cost, another vendor, or the budget you had in mind?" |
| "We tried something like this and it failed" | Risk aversion from real experience | "What went wrong last time?" Listen fully, then: "Here is specifically how this differs on that point" |
| "Can you just send a proposal?" | Skipping discovery to compare on price | "I want the proposal to fit your situation. Can I ask two more questions so I do not send you something generic?" |
| "Our team won't adopt this" | Change management concern | "That is the concern we hear most. Here is how [similar company] rolled it out: they started with [small scope] and expanded" |
| "We're going to build this in-house" | Belief that building is cheaper | "Some of our best customers started there. The question is usually not whether you can build it, but whether that team should spend six months on it instead of [their core work]" |
| "I'm not the right person" | Genuinely wrong contact, or deflecting | "Appreciate you saying so. Who would own a decision like this? Would you be open to making the introduction?" |

---

## Do not say

- Do not trash a competitor by name. Differentiate rather than denigrate, and a rep who attacks a competitor makes the buyer defend them.
- Do not say "we're the best at". Let the proof point do it.
- Do not raise pricing unless they do. If they ask early, give a range and redirect: "It depends on scope, so let me understand your situation first and I can give you an accurate number."
- Do not say "that's a great question" before every answer.
- Do not promise anything the business cannot deliver in order to win the deal.
- Do not describe something on the roadmap as though it exists today.
- Do not raise layoffs, negative press or a departure, even sympathetically.
