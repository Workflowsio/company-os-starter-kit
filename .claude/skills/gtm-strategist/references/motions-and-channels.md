# Motions and channels

Read at steps 3 and 4. Pick one primary motion and name the trigger for the second one.

---

## Motion 1: Outbound-led

**Best for:** a clear ICP, a deal size above roughly 5K, and a product that solves a problem the buyer already knows they have.

```
ICP definition -> account sourcing -> signal enrichment ->
multi-channel sequences -> meeting booked -> sales call -> proposal -> close
```

**Advantages:** predictable, scalable, fast feedback, and you control the volume.
**Risks:** it depends on data quality and copy, it feels spammy when done badly, and deliverability is a permanent maintenance job rather than a setup task.
**Add the second motion when:** outbound is producing consistent pipeline, around ten meetings a month. Then layer in content, which raises reply rates and shortens cycles rather than replacing anything.

---

## Motion 2: Inbound-led

**Best for:** companies with real content capability, an existing audience, or a product with natural search demand.

```
content creation -> search and social distribution -> site visit ->
lead capture -> nurture -> sales call -> close
```

**Advantages:** higher-quality conversations, it compounds, and it builds something competitors cannot copy quickly.
**Risks:** slow to start, hard to attribute, and it needs consistent output for months before the payoff. Most inbound programmes are killed at month four, one month before they would have worked.
**Add the second motion when:** inbound produces steady leads and you need either more volume or faster growth. Outbound is what gives you control of the pace.

---

## Motion 3: Product-led

**Best for:** software with a free tier or trial, lower deal sizes and high volume, where the product itself is the best argument.

```
signup -> activation -> usage milestones -> in-app prompts and email ->
self-serve upgrade or sales assist -> close
```

**Advantages:** low acquisition cost at scale, users convince themselves, and you can see exactly how they use it.
**Risks:** it needs real product investment, it does not work for high-value services, and free users consume support without converting.
**Add the second motion when:** self-serve works and larger accounts are stalling at the point where they need a human. Add sales assist for those, then outbound for the accounts that will never self-serve.

---

## Motion 4: Partner-led

**Best for:** a product that sits inside a larger ecosystem, or a market where trust and warm introductions decide deals.

```
partner identification -> co-selling agreement -> joint content and events ->
warm introduction -> sales call -> close
```

**Advantages:** it inherits existing trust, acquisition cost is low, and warm introductions close at high rates.
**Risks:** slow, hard to control, and entirely dependent on partner engagement. You cannot scale something you do not own.
**Add the second motion:** immediately. Partner-led should rarely be the only motion, so pair it with outbound or content from the start.

---

## Choosing the primary motion

| Factor | Outbound | Inbound | Product-led | Partner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Time to first revenue | 2 to 4 weeks | 3 to 6 months | 1 to 3 months | 3 to 6 months |
| Deal size sweet spot | mid to high | low to mid | low | mid to high |
| Team size needed | 1 to 3 | 2 to 4 | 3 to 5, including product | 1 to 2 |
| Scalability ceiling | moderate | high | very high | moderate |
| Dependency on data quality | high | low | medium | low |

Most B2B companies should start outbound-led. It is the fastest route to revenue, and it produces direct feedback on messaging, targeting and product fit, which the slower motions cannot give you for months. The exception is a self-serve product with real search demand.

## Channel selection

| Channel | Best for | Time to results | Team needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cold email** | Small to mid-market, high volume, measurable | 2 to 4 weeks | One person can run it |
| **Social outbound** | Mid-market to enterprise, relationship-driven | 4 to 8 weeks | One person can run it |
| **Cold calling** | Enterprise, complex sales, high deal size | Immediate | A dedicated calling team |
| **Content and inbound** | Any size, best long-term investment | 3 to 6 months to ramp | A writer plus distribution |
| **Paid social** | Enterprise account-based work, awareness and retargeting | 2 to 4 weeks | Budget plus a designer |
| **Paid search** | High-intent, bottom of funnel | 1 to 2 weeks | Budget plus landing pages |
| **Events and webinars** | Enterprise, relationship building | 4 to 8 weeks per event | Two or three people plus speakers |
| **Partnerships** | Any size, a multiplier on existing channels | 2 to 6 months | A dedicated partner owner |

Cost per meeting varies too much by market to publish as a fact. Measure your own within the first month and write the number down with the date next to it.

### The five questions

**1. What is your deal size?**
- Under 5K: cold email is the primary, social is optional, and events and enterprise ads are out.
- 5K to 50K: email plus social is the sweet spot. Add phone above 20K, and content as a multiplier.
- Above 50K: multi-channel is required, and you need air cover as well as direct outreach.

**2. Where does the buyer actually spend time?**
- Technical buyers: content, communities and developer events. Cold email works only when it is hyper-relevant.
- Business buyers in sales, marketing and operations: social and email, and they are the most responsive to outbound.
- Executive buyers: phone, warm introductions, events and visible thought leadership. Cold email alone rarely reaches them.

**3. How many target accounts exist?**
- Under 500: go deep. Account-based work, phone and social. Do not blast.
- 500 to 5,000: tier it. The top 50 get a personal touch and the rest get sequences.
- Over 5,000: volume outbound makes sense, email first with social on the top tier.

**4. What can the team staff?**
- One person: pick one channel and master it. Email usually proves fastest.
- Two people: a primary and a secondary, automating everything in between.
- Three or more: multi-channel orchestration becomes realistic.

**5. How fast do you need pipeline?**
- 30 days: cold email or cold calling. Nothing else moves fast enough.
- 90 days: email, social and early content.
- Six months or more: the full mix including content, search and partnerships.

### Four principles

1. **Start with one channel and master it.** Spreading across four from day one is the fastest way to get nothing from all four.
2. **Outbound plus content is the strongest early pairing.** Outbound drives pipeline now, content makes outbound convert better later.
3. **Layer channels by deal size**, not by ambition.
4. **Coordinate rather than silo.** Somebody who gets your email, sees your post and gets a message in the same week responds at a much higher rate than somebody who only gets the email, and it costs nothing extra to sequence them together.
