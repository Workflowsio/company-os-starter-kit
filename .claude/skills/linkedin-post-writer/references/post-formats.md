# Post formats and formatting rules

Read while choosing a format and while laying the draft out. One format per post. Each entry gives the structure, an example hook, when it works and the character target.

---

## 1. Hot take

A contrarian opinion that challenges something the category believes. It works through disagreement and through the reader thinking somebody finally said it.

```
[Bold contrarian statement, one sentence, 15 words maximum]

[One or two sentences of context: why most people are wrong]

[Three to five sentences building the argument, with specifics.
Name the tools, tactics or beliefs you are pushing back on.]

[One-line closer that reinforces the take, without hedging]

[CTA: a question that forces a stance]
```

Example hook: "Your CRM is not a strategy. It is a spreadsheet with a login page."

Use it for industry debates and for reframing something everyone takes for granted. It needs a strong opinion backed by experience, because contrarianism without evidence reads as attention-seeking and gets argued down in the comments.

Target: 500 to 800 characters. Hot takes lose their force when they ramble.

---

## 2. Framework

A step-by-step system. This is the core authority format, and it is what positions somebody as having done the work rather than having thought about it.

```
[Hook: the result the framework produces, or a bold claim about it]

[One or two lines of context: who this is for and why it matters]

[Numbered list, five to nine steps]
  - Each item: a clear title plus one or two lines of detail
  - Specific enough that a reader could start executing today

[One-line closer: the outcome of following it]

[CTA: a comment trigger for the full version, or a question]
```

Example hook: "Every outbound campaign we run follows the same seven steps. Here is the framework."

Use it for processes and systems you actually run. Real tool names, real numbers, real steps. A framework you have not run shows.

Target: 1,000 to 1,500 characters. This format earns its length through density rather than filler.

---

## 3. Story

A personal narrative that teaches through experience. Highest emotional engagement of any format.

```
[Hook: the most interesting moment, not the beginning.
Drop the reader into the middle of it.]

[Set the scene: two or three sentences. Where, when, what was at stake.]

[The turn: three to five sentences. What went wrong, what changed,
what you did not expect.]

[The lesson: two or three sentences the reader can apply to their own situation.]

[CTA: a question inviting a shared experience]
```

Example hook: "I applied for 300 jobs. Not a single reply. Then I sent one message."

Use it for founder moments, pivots, failures and things learned the hard way. The story has to serve the lesson, and a story that is only "look at me" should not be posted.

Target: 800 to 1,200 characters.

---

## 4. Tool review

An authoritative review of tools you have used. Credibility comes from the number tested and from being willing to say something negative.

```
[Hook: number of tools tested plus a verdict or a ranking]

[Brief context: why you tested them, what you were solving for]

[List: each tool with a one or two line verdict.
"Good UI" is useless. "Found 40 percent more valid emails than the next one" is useful.]

[Summary: your actual recommendation, with the caveat by use case]

[CTA: save this for your next stack review, or "what did I miss?"]
```

Example hook: "I tested 12 email finding tools over 90 days. Here is the honest ranking."

Only write this if you used the tools. Reading the landing page does not count, and the comments will find out. Check who you are naming against anything your business is not allowed to name publicly before this goes out.

Target: 800 to 1,500 characters.

---

## 5. Case study

Real results with enough detail to be credible. This is the format that converts followers into conversations.

```
[Hook: the headline metric. One number, one result.]

[Context: who, what, timeframe. "A B2B SaaS company with 50 employees" is fine
if the client cannot be named.]

[The approach: three to five bullets on what you actually did.]

[Results: specific numbers, before and after.]

[Takeaway: the one lesson that generalizes past this case]

[CTA: a question about their experience with the same problem]
```

Example hook: "47 qualified meetings in 60 days. Zero cold calls. Here is the system."

Every claim needs a number, and every named client needs permission. If you cannot put a number on it, it is not a case study.

Target: 800 to 1,200 characters.

---

## 6. Giveaway

Something free in exchange for engagement. High reach, moderate lead quality, and the comment trigger is the engine.

```
[Hook: what you are giving away and why it is worth having.
Lead with the outcome, not the format.]

[Two or three lines on what is inside, specifically.]

[Social proof: who else has used it and what it produced]

[CTA: "Comment [KEYWORD] and I will send it over"]
```

Example hook: "I am giving away our entire outbound playbook. The same one we run for clients."

The asset has to be genuinely useful. A disguised sales pitch gets noticed, and it costs you the next three posts. Maximum one a month, because more than that trains the audience to wait for free things rather than to read.

Target: 500 to 800 characters. The value is in the asset rather than the post.

---

## 7. Milestone

A business or personal milestone with enough specifics to be useful rather than a brag.

```
[Hook: the milestone as a contrast. Before and after is the strongest opener here.]

[Context: where you started, honestly. The worse the before, the better the story.]

[Three to five bullets on what actually happened: decisions, turning points,
the things that did not work]

[What you would do differently, or what surprised you]

[CTA: a question inviting others to share their own]
```

Example hook: "No clients. No team. That was eight months ago. Here is what happened."

Only works with real detail. "We grew a lot" is not a milestone post. Check your own public-content rules before putting a financial figure in one of these.

Target: 800 to 1,200 characters.

---

## Formatting rules

The feed is narrow, especially on a phone, and dense paragraphs die there.

**Line spacing.** A line break every one or two sentences, and no paragraph longer than two. A blank line between sections. This is the single most important formatting rule.

**Lists.** Plain numbers or number markers for three or more items. Each item is a number plus a title on one line, then one or two short bullets under it. No sub-bullets and no nesting. Seven or more items usually wants to be a carousel rather than a text post.

**The fold.** Roughly 210 characters show before "see more" on mobile. The hook and the first line of context have to fit inside that, because if the reader does not expand the post, the rest of it does not exist.

**Character targets by format**

| Format | Target | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Hot take | 500 to 800 | Short and sharp, loses force if it rambles |
| Framework | 1,000 to 1,500 | Earns its length through density |
| Story | 800 to 1,200 | Long enough for tension, short enough to hold |
| Tool review | 800 to 1,500 | Every line teaches something |
| Case study | 800 to 1,200 | Tight and metric-heavy |
| Giveaway | 500 to 800 | The value is in the asset |
| Milestone | 800 to 1,200 | Specifics rather than filler |

**General.** Dashes for bullets. Bold sparingly, for a section header or one key phrase, never for emphasis inside every sentence. No hashtags in the body, and three to five at the very end if you must. Never tag somebody for reach, only when they are directly referenced and will genuinely want to be. No links in the body, because they suppress reach; put the link in the first comment.
